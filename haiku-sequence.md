# A Season Turning

*A haiku sequence in six movements*

---

## I. Late Winter

Frost on the window,
a river trapped in the glass
waits for warmer days.

Snow settles gently,
each flake a tiny letter
the sky never sends.

Bare branch holds the moon,
patient as an old promise
nobody has kept.

Cold air in my lungs,
the whole world holding its breath
before the first thaw.

Footprints lead nowhere,
a trail that the wind erases
before I can follow.

The pond wears a lid,
silver and hard and silent,
keeping its secrets.

Smoke climbs from a chimney,
the only warm thing moving
in a still white world.

## II. First Green

Meltwater running,
finding its way down the hill
like a thought let go.

A single green shoot
pushes through the stubborn earth —
the stubbornness of hope.

Robins return home
to a yard they never left,
singing anyway.

The screen door opens,
then slams, the first loud sound
of the waking year.

Rain on the rooftop,
a thousand small fingertips
drumming out the spring.

Mud on my old boots,
the ground remembering how
to hold something loose.

A chickadee calls,
two notes the whole forest knows
and answers back.

Seeds in a small tray,
the first sun of the morning
warming the damp soil.

## III. Full Summer

Cicadas at noon,
the air vibrating with sound
too loud to ignore.

River stones are warm,
the water cool on my feet —
summer knows me well.

Tall grass in the field
bends to whatever wind comes,
never once complains.

Fireflies at dusk,
little lanterns drifting up,
brief and beautiful.

Sweat on my forehead,
the sun a heavy hand pressed
against the small of my back.

Children in the creek,
screaming with a joy so loud
it scares off the fish.

The porch swing creaking,
keeping time with the slow buzz
of a long afternoon.

## IV. Slow Gold

Leaves turn to lanterns,
the whole hillside burning bright
before it lets go.

Cool morning arrives,
a thin shawl around my shoulders,
summer's quiet goodbye.

Geese write their long V
across a page of pale sky,
heading somewhere south.

Apples in the grass,
sweet gifts the tree has given
freely to the ground.

Pumpkins on the porch,
orange as the dying light,
patient for the knife.

A sweater comes out,
smelling of cedar and dust
and last year's comfort.

Cornfields going brown,
the long rows bowing their heads
to the coming frost.

## V. First White

The first snow returns,
tiny strangers on my sleeve
melting into me.

Woodsmoke on the wind,
a familiar kind of ache
I cannot name well.

Long nights stretch outward,
the stars burning cold and clear
over frozen fields.

Breath visible now,
proof that I am still alive
in the shrinking light.

A dog barks once, twice,
then quiets, as if the dark
asked it to be still.

Icicles lengthen,
slow daggers of winter light
sharpening above.

Someone shovels snow,
the scrape of metal on stone
the block's only song.

## VI. Turning Again

One year folds over,
the same river, the same stone,
a different me.

I stand in the cold
and think of all the seasons
still waiting to come.

Boots by the back door,
dry mud cracking on the soles,
evidence of spring.

Nothing stays the same —
even stillness is a kind
of slow turning wheel.

The window still frosts.
The branch still holds up the moon.
The thaw always comes.

So I keep walking,
one foot after the other,
trusting the next spring.

Somewhere a green shoot
is already deciding
to break through the dark.

The frost will soften.
The hill will green and wither.
I will keep counting.
